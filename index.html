Model, equations and further reading

This tab solves the vertical cross-section under the gate of a high-electron-mobility transistor (HEMT), from the gate (left) into the substrate (right), self-consistently at 300 K. It then builds simple transistor characteristics from the computed channel charge. Equation numbers are local to the tab.

1 · Materials and band alignment

Composition-dependent parameters follow Vurgaftman, Meyer & Ram-Mohan. AlxGa1−xAs uses ΔEV = 0.53x eV against GaAs, with Γ and X valleys (X is lowest above x ≈ 0.45). Pseudomorphic InxGa1−xAs on GaAs uses ΔEC ≈ 0.65ΔEg. On InP, In0.52Al0.48As (Eg = 1.46 eV) and InxGa1−xAs give ΔEC ≈ 0.52 eV at the lattice-matched x = 0.53, rising to ≈0.63 eV for a strained In0.7Ga0.3As channel. Ga-face AlxGa1−xN on GaN uses ΔEC ≈ 0.7ΔEg plus a polarization sheet at each interface,

σpol = (Psp + Ppz)below − (Psp + Ppz)above ≈ 5.4×10¹³·x q/cm²(1)

A 1 nm AlN interlayer, as in most commercial GaN HEMTs, raises the local sheet and pushes the electrons away from the alloy-disordered AlGaN, which raises mobility. N-polar AlGaN uses the same parameters with every polarization reversed, so the 2DEG forms in GaN above an AlGaN layer. Lattice-matched In0.17Al0.83N (σ ≈ 2.7×10¹³ cm⁻², ΔEC ≈ 0.9 eV) and Sc0.18Al0.82N (σ ≈ 4.8×10¹³ cm⁻², ΔEC ≈ 1.1 eV, εr ≈ 14) are fixed-composition barriers; the ScAlN numbers are the least certain in the tab.

Other channels: β-(AlxGa1−x)2O3 with ΔEC ≈ 2x eV and m* ≈ 0.28 against Ga2O3; SixGe1−x on a relaxed Si0.2Ge0.8 buffer, interpolated so that strained Ge (x = 0) has ΔEV ≈ 0.15 eV to the buffer; and H-terminated diamond with its valence-band maximum at 4.2 eV below vacuum. AlxIn1−xSb uses Eg(InSb) = 0.17 eV, a valence-band offset of 0.41x eV and, for layers grown on a relaxed Al0.35In0.65Sb buffer, the biaxial-strain shifts described in the laser tab. SiN (χ ≈ 2.1 eV, εr 7.5), SiO2 (χ ≈ 0.95 eV, εr 3.9) and Al2O3 (χ ≈ 1.0 eV, εr 9) are gate dielectrics. A p-doped Al2O3 layer is a sheet of fixed negative charge, which stands in for the surface acceptors of transfer-doped diamond. At a dielectric/nitride interface the polarization charge is assumed to be compensated by interface donors, so no sheet appears there. Strain shifts of band gaps and the light-hole band are not included.

2 · Electrostatics and the quantized channel

Poisson's equation, d/dz(ε dφ/dz) = −q[p − n + ND+ − NA−] + dP/dz, is solved by Newton iteration on a mesh refined to 0.1 nm at interfaces. The Schottky gate fixes EC = EF,gate + qφB at the surface. In the layers ticked Q, electrons obey the BenDaniel–Duke equation,

−ħ²2ddz(1m*(z)dψdz) + EC(z)ψ = Eψ,   ns = Σi m*kTπħ²ln(1 + e(EF − Ei)/kT)(2)

and the two equations are iterated to self-consistency. The channel quasi-Fermi level is tied to the source (EF = 0); holes near the surface follow the gate, so no spurious hole inversion forms. In a p-GaN gate the hole quasi-Fermi level of the p-GaN and the barrier equals the gate's, as for an ohmic p-GaN contact.

For the p-channel exemplars the roles swap: holes are tied to the source, electrons near the gate follow the gate (and so do holes in an undoped gate barrier, as in the HIGFET), and the heavy-hole band is quantized with the same equation (EC → −EV, m* → mhh), giving ps = Σj (mhhkT/πħ²) ln(1 + e(Ej − EF)/kT). Only one hole band is used; light and split-off holes, and the strong non-parabolicity of the 2DHG, are not.

3 · Charge control

Above threshold the 2DEG and the gate form a parallel-plate capacitor,

qns ≈ εd + Δd(VG − VT)(3)

where d is the gate-to-channel distance (barrier plus spacer) and Δd ≈ 1–3 nm combines the wavefunction centroid and the finite density of states (quantum capacitance). Fig. 5's C–V view plots Cg = q·dns/dVG from the self-consistent sweep. In AlGaN/GaN devices VT is set mainly by the polarization sheet; in GaAs and InP devices by the δ-doping plane and the gate barrier; in the p-GaN gate by the ionized acceptors and the built-in voltage of the p-GaN/AlGaN/GaN junction.

4 · Transistor model (deliberately simple)

The drain current per unit gate width uses the gradual-channel approximation with the solved ns(VG) and a field-dependent velocity v(E) = μE/(1 + μE/vsat). Integrating along the channel from source to drain gives

ID(V′) = qμ ∫0V′ ns(VGS′ − V) dVLg + μV′/vsat(4)

The current saturates at the maximum of Eq. (4) over the drain-end channel voltage V′. Access resistances Rs = Rd are added in series (VGS′ = VGS − IDRs, V′ = VDS − 2IDRs). In the short-gate limit Eq. (4) approaches the velocity-saturated result ID ≈ qnsvsat and gm ≈ Cgvsat. The current-gain cutoff frequency is

fT ≈ gm2π(CgLg + Cpar)   →   vsat2πLg (intrinsic, saturated)(5)

Cpar lumps the fringing and pad capacitances that roughly halve fT in real devices. vsat here is an effective velocity under the gate, lower than the peak drift velocity of the bulk material; for the high-density nitride exemplars it is set lower still so that the currents land near measured values, standing in for self-heating. p-channel devices use the same model with ps against the gate drive −VGS, and Fig. 5 plots −ID against VGS and −VDS.

5 · What the model leaves out

  • Short-channel effects (drain-induced barrier lowering, output conductance), which need a 2D solver; the output curves are therefore perfectly flat in saturation.
  • Self-heating. The dissipated-power readout shows why it matters: GaN HEMTs at 10 V and 1 A/mm dissipate 10 W/mm.
  • Trapping and current collapse in GaN, DX centers in AlGaAs, gate leakage and breakdown.
  • Velocity overshoot and ballistic transport in sub-100 nm gates, and the field-plate and parasitic network that sets fmax.
  • Gate current: Schottky and p-GaN gates conduct at forward bias, which the VG ranges stop short of.
  • Buffers: graded metamorphic, strain-relief and SiGe virtual-substrate buffers are represented only by the relaxed layer at their top.

Model, equations and further reading

This tab solves the vertical cross-section of an edge-emitting laser at 300 K: the band diagram and quantum-well states under forward bias, the optical gain they provide, the guided optical mode, and from those the threshold and light–current curve. Equation numbers are local to the tab.

1 · Bands, strain and the operating point

The forward bias is applied as flat quasi-Fermi levels, EFn − EFp = qV, across the whole stack, which is accurate when the claddings block minority leakage. Poisson's equation and the BenDaniel–Duke equation for electrons and heavy holes (the layers ticked Q) are solved self-consistently, so injected carriers screen built-in fields, which matters for polar InGaN wells. Light holes are found on the converged potential. Biaxial strain in a well grown on a different lattice constant shifts the heavy-hole gap and splits the light holes off:

ΔEg,hh ≈ K ε,   Ehh − Elh ≈ 7 ε   (ε > 0 compressive)(1)

These are lumped deformation potentials: K = 5.5 eV for most arsenides and phosphides, falling to K ≈ 5.5 − 6x eV for InxGa1−xAs on GaAs because InAs has smaller deformation potentials; this reproduces 976 nm emission from In0.18 wells and ≈ 1.24 µm from In0.45 wells. Compressive strain puts the heavy holes on top and lightens their in-plane mass, which lowers the density needed for gain. The warning badge flags wells thicker than an approximate critical thickness 0.4 nm/ε.

2 · Optical gain

For each electron subband i and hole subband j, with envelope overlap Oij = |⟨ψe,i|ψh,j⟩|², reduced in-plane mass mr and Kane energy Ep, the modal gain is

Γg(ħω) = Σij q²ε0ħcEp Fpol mrnr ħω Oij Īij ∫ [fc(E′) − fv(E′)] L(ħω − E′) dE′(2)

where Īij is the mode intensity averaged over the pair (normalized so ∫|E|²dz = 1), Fpol = 1/4 for TE heavy holes, 1/12 for TE and 1/3 for TM light holes, and L is a sech line shape of width ħ/τin. Gain appears where fc > fv, which needs EFn − EFp to exceed the transition energy (Bernard–Duraffourg). Heavy holes give TE gain and light holes mostly TM, which is why compressive wells lase TE.

3 · Waveguide

The fundamental TE mode of the layer stack solves d²E/dz² + k0²n²(z)E = β²E (finite differences, largest eigenvalue). Indices come from the Afromowitz model for (Al,In)GaAs, the Broberg–Lindgren single oscillator for InGaAsP/InP, and a single oscillator fitted to GaN, AlN and InGaN near 450 nm. The confinement factor Γ is the share of |E|² inside the wells.

4 · Threshold and the light–current curve

Γgth = αi + αm,   αm = 12Lln1R1R2(3)

A background sweep raises EFn − EFp and records the peak modal gain and the recombination current J = q∫R dz over the whole stack, with radiative (Bnp), Auger (C(n+p)np) and Shockley–Read–Hall terms, including the confinement layers (Fig. 4). Threshold is where the gain reaches the loss. Above it the carrier density is clamped and

P1 = F1 ηi αmαi + αm hνq(I − Ith),   F1 = 1 − R1(1 − R1) + √(R1/R2)(1 − R2)(4)

5 · What the model leaves out

  • Valence-band mixing and non-parabolicity (k·p): one in-plane mass per hole band.
  • Current spreading and carrier diffusion in the stripe, so Ith = Jth·W·L.
  • Heating, gain compression and leakage over the barriers at high current: the L–I curve never rolls over.
  • Longitudinal modes, gratings (DFB, DBR) and lateral (ridge) guiding.
  • Vertical-cavity lasers (need a DBR/cavity solver) and quantum cascade lasers (need intersubband transitions); both are deferred.
